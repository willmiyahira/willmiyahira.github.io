Atom chips provide a versatile platform for trapping and manipulating
ultracold atoms close to a surface. In this project, I investigated
radio-frequency (RF) AC Zeeman potentials as an alternative to
conventional static magnetic trapping.

A central challenge for atom-chip traps is potential roughness arising
from imperfections in the current distribution of the chip wires.
I experimentally demonstrated that RF AC Zeeman traps can substantially
suppress this roughness compared with conventional DC magnetic traps.

I also investigated the RF AC Zeeman force as a tool for controlling
ultracold atoms, exploring its potential for precision manipulation
and atom-chip-based sensing.
